# Choosing where to walk, from the sweep alone

40 `behind` cases, `results/cases/cases_hard.json`, radius 1.5.  Two strategies
on the same signal; the second is implemented.

## The signal

Per sweep view: **how many of the instruction's own candidates it still sees** —
of the 10 reference slots ranked by p(subject class), how many correspond into
that view at cosine >= 0.80.

No predicate, no identity, no P-hat.  A candidate that does not correspond in a
view can never be selected from that view, so this is a necessary condition, and
it needs no idea which triplet is the right one.

## Strategy 1 — a curve over azimuth

Kernel-smooth the counts over azimuth (sigma 20), argmax over candidate
headings.  Its argmax sat on the +-30 boundary in 35 of 40 cases, and the peak's
POSITION is a median 20 degrees from the smallest heading that works.  So the
curve only ever answered "which side".  Kept as the figure (`plot_viewdist.py`),
not the policy.

## Strategy 2 — a side, then small steps  (implemented)

Compare the mean count over the sweep's left half against its right half, step
`--side-step` (10) degrees toward the better one, re-measure.  No kernel, no
sigma, no angle set.  `eval_move.py --bearing side`.

## What they score

Top-1 correct — `fuse_live.decide`'s rule, one real frame at the pose reached,
every case in the denominator.

| | stay | 1 step | 2 steps | 3 steps | best pose |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **strategy 2** | 13/40 | **32/40** | 20/40 | 5/40 | **36/40** |
| strategy 1, 30 deg steps | 13/40 | 32/40 | 9/40 | 0/40 | 32/40 |
| random heading | 13/40 | 16/40 | 4/40 | 1/40 | 18/40 |

Best fixed heading is 20/40, so the CHOICE of direction carries this, not the
fact of moving.  Re-running strategy 2 reproduced all 40 cases step-for-step.

**Only the first step earns anything** — the policy is `--steps 1` until there is
a stopping signal.  Small steps decay more slowly (20 against 9) and sample two
more poses, which is the whole of `best pose` 36 against 32.  The side never
flipped in 40 cases, so self-correction is not what produced that.

## Ruled out

| rule | top-1 correct |
|---|---|
| correspondence count, subject side (**used**) | 35/40 |
| `min` of the two ends | 37/40 |
| the two ends multiplied | 34/40 |
| deduplicated to distinct objects | 33/40 |
| deduplicated to distinct triplets | 31/40 |
| `min` class-probability gain over the triplet | 25/40 |
| within-view margin over rival subjects | 21/40 (inverted: 25) |
| view's own top-1 score, `rel * s * s` | 18/40 |
| agreement with the voted pair (`p_hat`) | 4/10 |

Three mechanisms behind those numbers.  Dedup loses because how many of an
object's boxes survive measures how clearly it is seen.  `margin` is inverted
because on the hidden side the target corresponds to nothing and the twin is
unopposed.  The vote fails on BIAS not splitting: dropping the grouping IoU from
0.5 to 0.1 took the winner's share 54% -> 66% and left accuracy unmoved.

`min` of the two ends is the next thing to try — it drops the assumption that the
subject is the hard end — but 2 cases in 40 is not evidence for changing now.

## Strategy 3 — score a view by its whole ranking

Each view ranks ~100 pairs and everything above reads only rank 1.  Weight each
pair by the standard deviation of its rank across the sweep, score a view by
`sum_k w_k / rank_k`.  Ranks only, so `rel`'s ~17-order drift cancels; no
predicate, no class label.  The instructed pair sits at the 99th percentile of
that weight, and not just by being rare -- over the views that DO score it, its
spread is still at the 93rd.

| | picks the right pair | AUC over views | its best view is right |
|---|---|---|---|
| top-1 vote / live-pair count | 20/38 | 0.76 | 26/38 |
| uniform weight (control) | — | 0.77 | 28/38 |
| **volatility-weighted** | **24/38** | **0.88** | **32/38** |
| reciprocal-rank fusion | 1/38 | | |

Fusion loses because it rewards a pair ranked high EVERYWHERE — the unoccluded
twin, which is what the vote also elects.  Displacement from the reference
ranking loses too (1–3 of 37): a view that reorders it is one that corresponded
to little.

**It does not move the policy.**  `--bearing volatility` scored 30/40 at one step
against 32/40, picking the same side in 38 of 40.  The policy consumes one bit,
and side choice was already 36/37; the ordering of views WITHIN a side is
exactly what `mean(left) vs mean(right)` throws away.  Kept as a mode, not
adopted.

## Stopping

"Stay if nothing beats here" — scoring the current pose through views 9 and 19,
which re-render it — fired on 2 of 13 correct poses and 2 of 25 wrong ones.  A
coin flip.

**The real frame's top-1 score does separate.**  Over 360 walked poses
(`probe_viewpoint_score.json`), a correct top-1 scores a median 1.05 decades
above a wrong one, AUC 0.77, best cut 74% against a 57% base rate.  The same
quantity on NVS-mapped views separates by 0.33 with the quartiles overlapping —
it was ruled out before because it was measured in the wrong place.

As a rule, `--stop-score -5.0` scores 27/40 against 32/40 for always stepping
once.  It is right when it speaks — 14/14 when it fires at step 1, 1/4 at step 0
— but it never fires in 19 of 40, and those walk to step 3 where accuracy is
2/40.  **The failure is the fallback, not the threshold.**  Successor, needing no
constant: walk the fixed steps, then return to the highest-scoring pose visited.
Worth 30/40 on these (truncated, lower-bound) trails.

Per-step columns are not comparable between a run with a stopping rule and one
without: an episode that stops early is absent from the later columns.

## A second scene type, and what it costs the score

`cases_slot` (40 cases, `build_slot.py`) hides the target behind a gap between
two occluders: the window is a median 15 degrees wide, both rims of the sweep are
85% more hidden than the best view, and there is NO same-class distractor.  So it
poses the opposite half of the problem — `cases_hard`'s target is visible but
indistinguishable from its twin, this one is simply invisible.  Ladder: 20/40 at
the start pose, 38/40 ceiling (`probe_viewpoint_slot.json`).

**Every ranking-based score is worthless here.**  Correlating each per-view score
against the hidden fraction the generator measured at that azimuth, over 6 cases:

| | `cases_slot` (hidden) | `cases_hard` (walked rank) |
|---|---|---|
| volatility | +0.00 | −0.36 |
| raw top-1 | +0.01 | −0.05 |
| live pairs | −0.12 | −0.41 |
| correspondence count | −0.33 | −0.60 |
| p(subject class) through the view | −0.40 | −0.02 |
| **sum of the standardised two** | **−0.39** | **−0.50** |

The two lists need different evidence, and the reason is structural: on
`cases_hard` the target and its twin are the same class by construction, so no
appearance score can separate them, and what changes with viewpoint is whether
the target corresponds at all.  On `cases_slot` there is nothing to disambiguate
and what changes is whether the object can be named.  Each score is blind on the
list it was not built for.

Adding the two standardised within a case is the only quantity measured that
speaks on both, and it costs almost nothing where the single best already worked.
It says a viewpoint helps in one of two ways — by making the object VISIBLE or by
making it DISTINGUISHABLE — and scores both.

Also settled here: the volatility score's 0.88 AUC on `cases_hard` was reading
SIDE, not viewpoint quality.  That list's curve is monotone in azimuth, so any
quantity carrying side information ranks views well; take the monotonicity away
and the correlation is 0.00.

## Open

* **Stopping.**  32 to 36 is entirely "when".  Curve height, argmax position,
  sign-flip and here-against-there each fail; the real frame's top-1 score is the
  first quantity that separates, and needs a better fallback than walking on.
* **The combined score is measured, not tried.**  Correlation on 6 cases per
  list; it has never driven a policy.
* **One predicate.**  `cases_slot` changes the scene type but not `behind`, and
  its single class pair (laptop / box) is one draw.  `cases_frozen.json` (59
  `on`, iTHOR) changes the predicate, and needs its own ladder first.
* **Why it works.**  "The target's slot returns" and "that side renders better"
  are indistinguishable here.  Recount with the target's slots removed.
* **Elevation is unused.**  The two views at one azimuth disagree 33% of the
  time, and tilting is free.
